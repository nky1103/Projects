## Alt text (carousel)
Cover (keyword-loaded, for Instagram Edit → alt text):
StartupSutra Startup Failure on ZestMoney, India's buy-now-pay-later poster child that promised card-less Indians financial freedom and shut down in 2023-24 from a $450 million peak. A fintech case study of why it failed: it did not lend its own money (bank and NBFC partners), did not control regulation (RBI's 2022 rule on credit lines cut off the model), and could not collect its defaults, then PhonePe's rescue acquisition fell through. A dependency and business-model lesson for founders.

1. Startup Failure, ZestMoney. It promised millions of Indians financial freedom, and had none itself; it didn't die because of one RBI rule.
2. Snapshot: ZestMoney, a buy-now-pay-later fintech needing no credit card, founded by Lizzie Chapman, Priya Sharma and Ashish Anantharaman, peaked at a $450 million valuation with Goldman Sachs backing, shut down 2023 to 2024.
3. The promise: only a few Indians have a credit card, so ZestMoney gave everyone credit, buy now and pay in instalments with no card, democratising credit for millions the banks ignored.
4. The catch: its freedom stood on things it did not control, it did not lend its own money (bank and NBFC partners), it did not control the rules, and it could not collect as defaults ran too high.
5. The trigger: in 2022 one RBI notification barred non-banks from loading credit lines onto prepaid instruments, cutting off the core of the BNPL model overnight, with no say and no plan B.
6. The end: PhonePe was set to buy it for $200 to 300 million then pulled out after due diligence, the founders stepped down, and in 2023 to 2024 ZestMoney shut down from a $450 million peak to zero.
7. The lesson: freedom built on someone else's rails isn't freedom; own your economics, your rails and your risk, or a single rule change can end you. Follow @statupsutra, Startup Failure on Mondays.
