India's next gold mine isn't underground. It's in your drawer.

Every year India throws away over 1.6 million tonnes of electronics. Around 90% is handled by the informal sector, where the gold, copper and lithium inside get burned off or lost. Then India imports the same minerals back.

Attero Recycling looked at that broken industry and saw a business.

Founded in 2008 by brothers Nitin and Rohan Gupta, Attero isn't really a recycler. It's a technology company that mines critical minerals from dead electronics and batteries, recovering gold, copper, lithium, cobalt and nickel at close to 99% purity.

Here's the strategy most people miss.

Anyone can collect scrap. The moat is the extraction. Attero spent 15 years building 46 patented recovery processes, so it owns the one step competitors can't cheaply copy. That is the whole business.

Then the tailwinds arrived. EPR rules now force brands to recycle 60, 70, then 80% of their e-waste. The EV boom needs lithium India doesn't mine. Demand became mandatory.

The result: revenue from ₹440 Cr to over ₹1,000 Cr, cash-flow positive, and IPO-bound.

The Founder Playbook:
Find opportunity in a broken industry → Make the patent your moat, not the plant → Turn regulation into a tailwind → Own the technology layer, not just operations.
