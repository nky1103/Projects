Case Study #002 · Sunday

Attero Recycling. India's urban gold mine. The full write-up.

Series · Case Study (Sun) Date · 26 Jul 2026 Subject · Attero Recycling Framework · Own the technology layer

01 Carousel caption (posts 08:00 IST)

India's next gold mine isn't underground. It's in your drawer. Every year India throws away over 1.6 million tonnes of electronics. Around 90% is handled by the informal sector, where the gold, copper and lithium inside get burned off or lost. Then India imports the same minerals back. Attero Recycling looked at that broken industry and saw a business. Founded in 2008 by brothers Nitin and Rohan Gupta, Attero isn't really a recycler. It's a technology company that mines critical minerals from dead electronics and batteries, recovering gold, copper, lithium, cobalt and nickel at close to 99% purity. Here's the strategy most people miss. Anyone can collect scrap. The moat is the extraction. Attero spent 15 years building 46 patented recovery processes, so it owns the one step competitors can't cheaply copy. That is the whole business. Then the tailwinds arrived. EPR rules now force brands to recycle 60, 70, then 80% of their e-waste. The EV boom needs lithium India doesn't mine. Demand became mandatory. The result: revenue from ₹440 Cr to over ₹1,000 Cr, cash-flow positive, and IPO-bound. 03 Alt text 9 slides

  1. 1. Case Study, Attero Recycling. India's next gold mine isn't underground, it's in your drawer. Dead electronics in, gold, copper and lithium out.
  2. 2. The problem: India generates over 1.6 million tonnes of e-waste a year, ~90% handled informally, so critical minerals are lost and then re-imported.
  3. 3. The business model: dead electronics and batteries in, proprietary processing at 99% purity, recovered minerals out, sold to OEMs and battery makers.
  4. 4. The opportunity: India's e-waste market grows from 3.3 to 10 billion dollars by 2034, EPR mandates reach 80% by FY28, and the EV boom needs lithium.
  5. 5. Go-to-market: sell to those the law forces to recycle, via OEM partnerships, enterprise metal supply, a collection network, and battery makers.
  6. 6. The framework: own the technology layer, not operations. Collection and sorting are commoditised; the patented 99% purity recovery step is the moat.
  7. 7. Competitive landscape: Attero (e-waste + batteries, 46 patents, carbon credits) vs Lohum (batteries), Recykal (marketplace), and the informal sector.
  8. 8. Why they won: technology (46 patents), timing (before the EV wave), regulation (EPR demand), execution (₹440 Cr to ₹1,000 Cr+, cash-flow positive).
  9. 9. Founder playbook: find opportunity in a broken industry, make the patent the moat, turn regulation into a tailwind, own the technology layer. Follow @statupsutra.
