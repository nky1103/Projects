Everyone thinks legal AI means robot lawyers. NYAI just raised $1.5 million to fix something far more boring, and far more valuable.

NYAI, a Pune startup founded in 2025 by Chinmay Bhosale and Vikrant Labde, builds AI for legal research and compliance, tuned to Indian law. It raised a $1.5 million seed from family offices before it has scaled. So what's the signal they saw?

Signal one, the pain: every company, law firm and listed business in India drowns in statutes, contracts and filings. Slow, costly, and locked behind expensive experts.

Signal two, the wave: AI can finally read and reason over dense legal text, with citations. Point a rising technology at a document-heavy market and you have a wedge.

At seed there are no metrics, so investors buy the founders, the wave and the size of the pain. NYAI picked the grunt work everyone hates and everyone needs.

The seed signal: the best bet is often the most boring wedge into a massive, painful market.
