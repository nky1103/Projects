1. Founder Framework, Dr. V. Arthritis left his hands so twisted he couldn't hold a pen, yet he built the world's largest eye hospital.
2. Snapshot: Dr. G. Venkataswamy, known as Dr. V, built the Aravind Eye Care System, starting in 1976 at age 58 with an 11-bed clinic, no money and no plan, with a mission to end needless blindness.
3. The struggle: at 30, rheumatoid arthritis left him bedridden for over a year with fingers too crippled to hold a pen, so he taught himself to hold a scalpel again and became a cataract surgeon.
4. Framework one, engineer compassion like an operation: he asked if McDonald's can sell billions of burgers, why not millions of sight-restoring surgeries, and turned cataract surgery into a high-volume, high-quality, low-cost assembly line, over 100 a day.
5. Framework two, cross-subsidy: no one is ever turned away, paying patients fund the roughly 60 percent treated free or steeply subsidised, and the hospital still runs a surplus with no dependence on donations.
6. The impact: over 100,000 surgeries by his own crippled hands, millions given their sight back, and Aravind grew into the world's largest eye-care network, roughly 60 percent cared for free yet profitable.
7. The framework: a mission scales only when you engineer it like a business; Dr. V made caring efficient enough to reach millions, and purpose and profit funded each other. Follow @statupsutra, Founder Framework on Saturdays.
