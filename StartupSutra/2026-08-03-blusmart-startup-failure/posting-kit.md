1. Startup Failure, BluSmart. Riders loved it, then it vanished overnight, and it didn't die because electric cars don't work.
2. Snapshot: BluSmart, an all-electric ride-hailing service and Uber rival across Delhi NCR, Mumbai and Bengaluru, co-founded by Anmol Jaggi, Puneet Jaggi and Punit K Goyal, shut down in April 2025.
3. The twist: the product was good, clean electric cars, no surge pricing, fixed fares, salaried drivers and loyal riders, with around 10,000 drivers depending on it. Demand was never the problem.
4. The fatal structure: BluSmart did not own its cars, its entire fleet was leased from Gensol, a separate listed company run by the same co-founder, putting one critical dependency in related-party hands.
5. What actually happened: Gensol took around ₹978 crore in loans to buy EVs, and SEBI alleged over ₹200 crore was diverted to promoter-linked entities, even a luxury apartment, then barred the Jaggi brothers from the markets.
6. The domino: with Gensol frozen, BluSmart lost access to its fleet, could not operate, stopped services and stranded around 10,000 drivers. The product was fine, the structure and governance killed it.
7. The lesson: a great product cannot survive bad governance, never let a related party control the one thing you depend on. Follow @statupsutra, Startup Failure on Mondays.
