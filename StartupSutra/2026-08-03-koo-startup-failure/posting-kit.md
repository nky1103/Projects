1. Startup Failure, Koo. 60 million users, ministers on board, then it was gone; and it didn't die because it couldn't beat Twitter.
2. Snapshot: Koo, a multilingual made-in-India Twitter rival founded by Aprameya Radhakrishna and Mayank Bidawatka, peaked near a $275 million valuation and shut down in July 2024.
3. The rise: in 2021 India clashed with Twitter, and ministers, celebrities and lakhs of users jumped to the made-in-India app overnight, downloads went vertical.
4. The flaw: it was built on a moment, not a business; a download spike isn't a business, and when the news cycle moved on, so did the users.
5. The economics: Koo raised about $50 million, hit a $275 million paper valuation and 60 million-plus downloads, but never built a real revenue model, and social media's server and moderation costs are brutal.
6. The end: funding froze, buyers walked because no one wanted the cost and liability of moderating user content, merger talks collapsed, salaries went unpaid from April 2024, and Koo shut in July 2024.
7. The lesson: a moment is not a moat; growth you don't control with no path to revenue is rented, not owned, so build the business, not just the buzz. Follow @statupsutra, Startup Failure on Mondays.
