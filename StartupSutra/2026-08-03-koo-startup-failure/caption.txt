Koo had 60 million users, cabinet ministers posting daily, and a valuation near $275 million. Two years later it was gone. And it didn't die because it couldn't beat Twitter.

Koo was a made-in-India, multilingual answer to Twitter, built by Aprameya Radhakrishna and Mayank Bidawatka. In 2021, when the Indian government clashed with Twitter over blocking certain accounts, ministers and celebrities publicly jumped to Koo overnight. A patriotic wave carried it. Downloads went vertical. It looked unstoppable.

Here's the problem. It was built on a moment, not a business.

A download spike isn't a business. The users came because of a news cycle, not because Koo had something they couldn't live without. When the moment passed, so did the growth.

And the economics never worked. Social media is brutally expensive, servers and content moderation cost a fortune, and Koo never cracked how to make money from any of it. It raised around $50 million, hit a paper valuation of $275 million, crossed 60 million downloads, and still had no real revenue model.

When the funding winter hit, it had nothing to fall back on. Buyers looked, then walked, because no one wanted the cost and legal liability of moderating user-generated content. Merger talks collapsed. Salaries went unpaid from April 2024. Koo shut down in July 2024.

The buzz was always real. The business underneath it never was.

The lesson for founders is hard but clean: a moment is not a moat. Growth you don't control, with no path to revenue, is rented, not owned. Build the business, not just the buzz.
