1. Startup Failure, Quibi. How do you burn $1.75 billion and die in 6 months. Raised 1.75 billion, alive 6 months, sold for under 100 million.
2. Snapshot: Quibi, founded 2018 in Los Angeles by Jeffrey Katzenberg and Meg Whitman, raised $1.75B from Disney, Sony, Alibaba, Goldman; premium short-form video, Apr to Dec 2020.
3. The opportunity: short-form video was exploding, so Quibi put Hollywood on your phone with big stars and ten-minute episodes. On paper it sounded perfect.
4. The product: premium episodes under 10 minutes, mobile only, a paid $4.99 to $7.99 subscription, and Turnstyle rotation. Optimised for the product they imagined.
5. What went wrong: paid in a world of free, mobile-only with no TV, no clips or sharing, launched into COVID, and it ignored the creator economy.
6. Consumer behavior won: TikTok (free, social, creator-led), YouTube (free, any screen), Netflix (TV, binge) vs Quibi (paid, mobile-only, no sharing). Every habit was already formed.
7. Assumption vs reality. Assumption: people want premium Hollywood content on the go. Reality: people wanted free, shareable, creator-led content at home.
8. The fatal mistake: they optimised for production quality, not consumer behavior. Quibi built the future they imagined; TikTok built the future people actually wanted.
9. Founder playbook: product-market fit beats funding, distribution matters as much as content, listen to behavior not assumptions, premium isn't value, simplicity wins. Follow @statupsutra, Startup Failure on Mondays.
