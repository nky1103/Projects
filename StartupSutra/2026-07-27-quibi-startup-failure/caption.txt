How do you burn $1.75 billion and shut down in six months?

Meet Quibi. Backed by Jeffrey Katzenberg and Meg Whitman, funded by Disney, Sony, Alibaba and Goldman Sachs. On paper, unstoppable.

The pitch sounded perfect. Short-form video was exploding, so Quibi put Hollywood on your phone: big stars, big budgets, episodes under ten minutes, made for your commute.

It launched in April 2020. It was dead by December.

Here's the part most people miss. Quibi wasn't a bad product. It was beautifully built. It was solving the wrong problem.

Every choice was optimised for the product they imagined, not the way people actually watch. It was paid, in a world of free. Mobile-only, so you couldn't cast it to your TV. It had no clips and no sharing, so nothing could go viral. And it launched into COVID, right as the commute it was built for disappeared.

While Quibi bet on Hollywood, TikTok bet on creators. One assumed what people should want. The other watched what they actually did.

The assumption: people want premium Hollywood content, on the go.
The reality: people wanted free, shareable, creator-led content, at home.

Quibi built the future they imagined. TikTok built the future people actually wanted.

The Founder Playbook:
Product-market fit beats funding → Distribution matters as much as content → Listen to behavior, not boardroom assumptions → Premium doesn't always create value → Simplicity wins.
