Everyone thinks India's growth capital chases apps. $40 million just went into a company that machines metal for jet engines.

Raghu Vamsi Aerospace, a Hyderabad company founded by Vamsi Vikas, just raised a $40 million Series B co-led by Norwest and Skegen. It makes precision-engineered components for aircraft and defence systems. No app, no consumer brand, just deep, capital-heavy manufacturing.

So why did serious growth investors write that cheque?

Because the world's top aerospace names already buy from it: GE Aerospace, Safran, Honeywell, Collins. And that's the whole point. Getting qualified to supply a jet-engine maker takes years of audits, testing and certification. Once you're in, you're extremely hard to replace. That certification is the moat, and it's one you can't copy in a weekend.

This is what growth capital is for. It isn't seed money betting on an idea. It's fuel to scale a base that already works. The $40 million goes into more capacity across its 10-plus facilities in India, the UK and the US, and a new integrated campus near Hyderabad airport.

And it's riding a powerful wave. Rising global defence budgets, the world's "China plus one" hunt for new suppliers, and Make in India are all pointing at exactly this. Right supplier, right moment.

The lesson for founders: growth capital doesn't only chase software. It funds the hard-to-build supply chain behind a boom. A moat that takes years to build, riding a decade-long tailwind, is exactly what growth investors want.
