The world thinks the smart ring is a Silicon Valley gadget. In India, a homegrown startup outsells every global brand. Here's how Ultrahuman did it.

Ultrahuman, founded by Mohit Kumar in Bengaluru, makes a health-tracking smart ring. In India it holds around 30% of the smart-ring market, rank one, ahead of Finland's Oura and even Samsung. And it runs at roughly a $150 million annual revenue run-rate.

The surprising part: it didn't win by out-inventing anyone. It won on two edges most people miss.

Edge one: it isn't really selling a ring. The ring is just the wedge, the thing you wear 24/7. On top of it sits a metabolic layer (glucose sensing, blood markers, recovery) and, underneath, years of your health data. That data is the moat. Once it knows your body, leaving is expensive.

Edge two: every ring is made in India. While rivals import, Ultrahuman iterates faster and controls its costs at home. Manufacturing isn't a footnote, it's the advantage.

Now the honest part. Winning at home is not the same as winning the world. Oura holds two-thirds of the global market and sued Ultrahuman in the US, hurting its Ring Air there. Then Oura entered India. The category's own shipments dipped. This is a real fight, on both sides.

The takeaway for founders: you don't have to invent the category to win it. Find the edge nobody else has, in Ultrahuman's case, home manufacturing plus locked-in data, and win there first.
