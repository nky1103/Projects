1. Case Study, Ultrahuman. The world thinks the smart ring is a Silicon Valley gadget; in India a homegrown startup outsells them all.
2. Snapshot: Ultrahuman, based in Bengaluru and Gurugram, founded by Mohit Kumar, makes smart rings in India, running at about a $150 million revenue run-rate, competing with Oura and Samsung.
3. The anomaly: India's number-one smart ring is not Oura or Samsung, it's Ultrahuman, at around 30 percent share, and every ring is built in India.
4. The real moat: it isn't selling a ring, it's building a health operating system, the ring is the wedge, the metabolic layer adds glucose and blood markers, and years of your data is the moat.
5. Why it wins at home: around 30 percent India share at rank one, about a $150 million run-rate, a ₹400 crore Series C in March 2026, and 100 percent of rings manufactured in India.
6. The hard part: winning at home is not winning the world; Oura holds two-thirds globally and sued Ultrahuman in the US, then entered India, and category shipments dipped.
7. The takeaway: you don't have to invent the category, win where you have an unfair edge, home manufacturing plus locked-in data. Follow @statupsutra, Case Study on Sundays.
