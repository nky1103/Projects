1. Case Study, Skyroot. For 50 years reaching orbit in India was a government job; two ex-ISRO scientists just did it privately.
2. Snapshot: Skyroot Aerospace, based in Hyderabad, founders Pawan Chandana and Bharath Daka (ex-ISRO), reached orbit with Vikram-1 in July 2026, India's first space unicorn at a $1.1 billion valuation.
3. The breakthrough: India opened space to private players in 2020 and Skyroot reached orbit first, making India the third country ever, after the US and China, with a private company launching to orbit.
4. The real edge: over 90 percent of the rocket is made in India, with its own engines, 3D-printed parts and avionics, vertically integrated and not import-dependent, strategic autonomy not just a cost play.
5. The business: a $1.1 billion valuation, about $60 million raised from Sherpalo and GIC, 1,000-plus employees, targeting a cadence of one launch a month, cheap and repeatable made-in-India launch.
6. Why it matters: space is strategic, running communications, defence, navigation and imaging, and a home-grown launch capability means India isn't renting foreign rockets to reach its own orbit.
7. The takeaway: self-reliance isn't a slogan, it's a moat; build the hard capability at home and you own the future instead of renting it. Follow @statupsutra, Case Study on Sundays.
