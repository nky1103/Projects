For 50 years, reaching orbit in India was a government job. Two ex-ISRO scientists just broke that open.

Skyroot Aerospace, founded by Pawan Chandana and Bharath Daka, sent its Vikram-1 rocket to orbit in July 2026. That made India only the third country, after the US and China, with a private company launching to orbit. And over 90% of the rocket is made in India.

This isn't just a cool startup. It's strategic self-reliance. Space runs communications, defence, navigation and imaging, and India no longer has to rent foreign rockets to reach its own orbit.

The lesson: self-reliance isn't a slogan, it's a moat. Build the hard capability at home and you own the future instead of renting it.
