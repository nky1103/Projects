Everyone chases quick commerce for food. This startup raised $5.5 million to deliver plywood and paint. Before it sold a single order.

Fixxly, a Bengaluru startup founded by Shezan Bhojani and Sachith Varma, just raised a $5.5 million seed round from Accel, Fireside Ventures and Lightspeed. It does 30-minute delivery of building materials: plywood, paint, electricals, lighting, hardware, plumbing.

Here's the anomaly. It raised before it even launched. No revenue, no customers, commercial launch still weeks away. So what did some of India's sharpest investors actually see?

Signal one: the market. Contractors, plumbers, painters and electricians lose hours sourcing basic supplies from a fragmented, chaotic, unglamorous market. Boring and painful is exactly what makes it a big opportunity, because nobody glamorous is fighting for it.

Signal two: the wedge and the wave. Take the quick-commerce playbook that already works for food, and point it at B2B essentials instead. AI matches real-time inventory, proximity and demand across local dark stores to deliver in about 30 minutes, riding India's construction and infrastructure boom. Right playbook, right market, right time.

And here's the part most people miss. At seed, there is nothing to measure. No sales, no growth curve, no cohort data. So investors don't buy numbers. They buy the founders, the size of the problem, and the timing, before a single order ships.

That's the seed signal: a sharp wedge into a broken market, plus the wave behind it. The most fundable seed idea is often the most boring one, aimed at a market everyone else overlooks.
