How does a startup with barely a product raise $15 million?

Vorflux just did. And the answer is the most important lesson in early-stage investing.

Vorflux is building an AI autopilot for software engineering. It turns a ticket into a finished, mergeable pull request: it plans the work, spins up sub-agents, writes the code, runs the tests, and reviews itself.

The product is early. There's no revenue. So why did Y Combinator and Peak XV, plus angels like Parker Conrad and Balaji Srinivasan, write a $15 million seed cheque, the size of a Series A?

Because at seed, there are no metrics to buy. You're not funding a product. You're funding two things.

One: the founder. Prasanna Sankar co-founded Rippling and ran its engineering, building it into a $13 billion company. Investors have watched him build before.

Two: the wave. AI coding agents are the hottest category in software right now. A proven builder, pointed at a rising market, before it gets crowded.

Here's the part most people miss. His last startup, a Web3 venture, shut down. Investors backed him anyway. A proven builder in a hot category outweighs one miss.

That's the seed signal: track record plus timing. When you have no numbers, those are the only two things worth betting on.
