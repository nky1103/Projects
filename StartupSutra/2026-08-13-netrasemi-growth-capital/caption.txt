Everyone thinks India can't make its own chips. ₹107 crore just backed a startup proving them wrong.

Netrasemi, a Kerala startup, raised a ₹107 crore Series A led by Zoho and Unicorn India Ventures to build edge-AI chips, with its own IP, designed in India.

Why does this matter? India writes world-class software, but imports almost every chip that runs its phones, cars, cameras and defence. Depending on other countries for all of it is a national weak spot.

Here's why serious money backed it. Netrasemi owns its own chip IP, the hardest thing to copy. Its A2000 chip powers smart cameras and edge devices, and the government is co-backing it under the chip-design scheme. Real silicon IP takes years, and that's the moat.

Growth capital here isn't funding an app. It's funding the years and the fabs it takes to make chips at home, riding a national semiconductor mission and an edge-AI boom.

The lesson: growth capital is starting to back India's hardest frontier, the chip itself. Self-reliance is the thesis.
